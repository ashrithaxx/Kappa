Glossary & Formulas

Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM)

The stochastic differential equation driving every simulated price path, and its exact discretization used on the grid (no Euler bias, only Monte Carlo sampling error). Drift μ pulls the price up or down on average over time, while volatility σ scales the size of the random shocks W_t, so higher σ produces wider, noisier paths around that drift.

$$dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t$$
$$S_{t+\Delta t} = S_t \exp\!\left[\left(\mu - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)\Delta t + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z\right],\quad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

Black-Scholes Closed Form

The analytical benchmark the Monte Carlo option price is checked against, under the same risk-neutral assumptions. d1 and d2 measure how far the current price sits from the strike in volatility-adjusted, risk-neutral terms; N(·) is the standard normal cumulative distribution, so N(d2) is roughly the probability the option finishes in the money.

$$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0/K) + \left(r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}, \qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$$
$$C = S_0 N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2) \qquad\qquad P = K e^{-rT} N(-d_2) - S_0 N(-d_1)$$

Monte Carlo Option Price

The discounted average payoff across simulated terminal prices, with a standard error that shrinks as more paths are added, this is the convergence shown against Black-Scholes. Because SE shrinks with 1/√M, cutting the error in half requires roughly 4x as many simulated paths, which is why the convergence chart flattens out slowly at high path counts.

$$\hat{C} = e^{-rT} \cdot \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M} \max\!\left(S_T^{(i)} - K,\ 0\right), \qquad \text{SE} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{payoff}}}{\sqrt{M}}$$

Value at Risk (VaR) & Expected Shortfall (ES)

VaR is the loss threshold not expected to be exceeded at a given confidence level, ES is the average loss in the tail beyond that threshold. Both are reported as positive numbers representing a loss.

$$\text{VaR}_\alpha = -\,\text{Quantile}_{1-\alpha}(\text{P\&L}) \qquad \text{ES}_\alpha = -\,\mathbb{E}\left[\text{P\&L} \mid \text{P\&L} \le -\text{VaR}_\alpha\right]$$

The parametric method assumes Normally distributed P&L as an independent analytical check, divergence from the historical (simulated) figures reflects skew and fat tails the Monte Carlo engine captures.

$$\text{VaR}_\alpha^{\text{param}} = -\left(\mu_{\text{P\&L}} + z_{1-\alpha}\,\sigma_{\text{P\&L}}\right)$$

Risk Contribution (Euler Allocation)

Splits total portfolio VaR across assets by each asset's covariance with the portfolio's P&L, contributions sum exactly to the total VaR. β_i captures how much each asset co-moves with total portfolio losses, an asset can carry outsized risk contribution relative to its size if it's highly correlated with the rest of the book.

$$\text{VaR}_i = \beta_i \cdot \text{VaR}_{\text{total}}, \qquad \beta_i = \frac{\text{Cov}(\text{P\&L}_i,\ \text{P\&L}_p)}{\text{Var}(\text{P\&L}_p)}$$

Stress Testing Shocks

Each scenario perturbs the baseline portfolio parameters before re-running the same simulation and risk pipeline. Price shocks move S0 directly, volatility and drift shocks scale or shift σ and μ, and correlation shocks nudge ρ while keeping it clipped to a valid [-1,1] range, letting you see how VaR and expected value react to each stress independently.

$$S_0' = S_0 (1 + \text{shock}) \qquad \sigma' = \sigma \cdot m \qquad \mu' = \mu + \Delta\mu \qquad \rho' = \text{clip}(\rho + \Delta\rho,\ -1,\ 1)$$
